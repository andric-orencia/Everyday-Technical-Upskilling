# Foundations

The computer-science and math base that a CS degree gives you — rebuilt here for a self-taught path into AI/ML engineering.

> **Why this track exists:** You came from manufacturing engineering, and you can already *build* AI automations. What a CS background adds is the ability to explain *why* things work, reason about tradeoffs, and pass technical interviews. That's what this track fills in.

Don't try to master everything here before moving on. Follow the [`ROADMAP.md`](../ROADMAP.md) — it interleaves these foundations with hands-on AI work so concepts stick.

---

## The three pillars

| Pillar | Folder | Why it matters for AI/ML |
|--------|--------|--------------------------|
| **Python** | [`Python/`](./Python/) | The language of AI/ML. Fluency here makes everything else faster. |
| **Computer Science** | [`Computer-Science/`](./Computer-Science/) | How software actually runs — OS, networking, databases, Git, complexity. Interviewers assume you know this. |
| **Math for ML** | [`Math-for-ML/`](./Math-for-ML/) | Just enough linear algebra, probability, and calculus to understand embeddings, attention, and training. |

---

## How to study foundations

1. **Learn just-in-time.** Don't binge theory. When the AI track mentions "embeddings," come read the vectors section here. Context makes it stick.
2. **Explain it out loud.** If you can't explain a concept in plain words, you don't own it yet. Write the explanation in your own words in these READMEs.
3. **Connect to what you already know.** You've reasoned about physical systems, tolerances, and process control as a manufacturing engineer — much of CS (queues, throughput, bottlenecks, feedback loops) maps directly onto that intuition.

---

## Priority for the 3-month timeline

If time is tight, this is the order of impact:

1. **Python fluency** — non-negotiable, used every single day.
2. **Big-O + core data structures** — directly tested in DSA rounds.
3. **How the web works** (HTTP, APIs, client/server) + **databases/SQL** — needed for system design and real AI apps.
4. **OS basics** (process/thread/memory) — comes up when discussing inference and serving.
5. **Math for ML** — light touch; enough to not be lost, not a math degree.
