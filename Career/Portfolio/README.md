# Portfolio & Online Presence

For a career-changer, your public presence *is* your credential. When a recruiter or hiring manager searches your name, GitHub and LinkedIn should tell a clear story: **an engineer who builds AI.**

---

## GitHub profile

Your GitHub is your live resume for engineering roles.

- [ ] **Profile README** — a short intro: who you are, what you're building, your focus (AI/ML). Pin your best repos.
- [ ] **Pinned repositories** — your 2–3 best projects, each with a strong README.
- [ ] **Green squares** — steady contribution history shows consistency (this repo counts — commit your practice).
- [ ] **Clean repos** — good READMEs, no committed secrets, meaningful commit messages.
- [ ] **This upskilling repo** — public, it demonstrates discipline and a learning mindset.

---

## LinkedIn

- [ ] Headline that states your target: e.g., "Engineer | Building applied AI/ML | ex-Manufacturing Engineer"
- [ ] About section telling your transition story (pull toward AI, evidence of building)
- [ ] Projects & skills listed; link your GitHub and portfolio
- [ ] Post occasionally about what you're learning/building — visibility compounds
- [ ] Connect with people in AI/ML roles and communities

---

## Portfolio (optional but strong)

A simple portfolio site or a single well-structured GitHub README that showcases:

- [ ] A one-line pitch of who you are
- [ ] Your best 2–3 projects with links, screenshots/demos, and the problem each solves
- [ ] Your skills and stack
- [ ] Contact + resume link

Keep it simple. A clean GitHub profile README can *be* your portfolio — don't over-engineer this.

---

## The story your presence should tell

Anyone landing on your profiles in 30 seconds should conclude:

> "This person came from manufacturing engineering, taught themselves AI, and has actually shipped LLM/RAG/agent projects. They understand the fundamentals and they build."

Every item above should reinforce that sentence.
