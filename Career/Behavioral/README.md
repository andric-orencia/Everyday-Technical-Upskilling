# Behavioral Interviews

The "tell me about a time…" round. It's often underestimated and it's very winnable — especially for you, because a career-change into AI/ML is a compelling story if you prepare it. Structured, specific answers with real numbers beat vague ones every time.

---

## The STAR framework

Answer every behavioral question in four beats:

- **S — Situation:** briefly set the context (1–2 sentences).
- **T — Task:** what you were responsible for / the goal.
- **A — Action:** what *you* specifically did (the bulk of the answer — use "I," not "we").
- **R — Result:** the outcome, quantified where possible, plus what you learned.

Keep each story to ~2 minutes. Practice out loud until it's natural, not memorized.

---

## Build your story bank

Prepare **5–7 stories** you can flex to many questions. Draw heavily from your manufacturing career *and* your AI projects. Store them here (e.g., `stories.md`) using the template below.

### Story template

```
Title: <short label, e.g., "Automated service remediation">

Question types it answers: <e.g., initiative, problem-solving, technical challenge>

S: <context>
T: <your goal/responsibility>
A: <what you specifically did — steps, decisions, tradeoffs>
R: <result with numbers + what you learned>
```

### Stories every candidate should have ready

- [ ] A technical problem you solved (ideally an AI project or automation)
- [ ] A time you took initiative / went beyond your role
- [ ] A failure or mistake and what you learned
- [ ] A time you had to learn something hard, fast (your whole AI transition!)
- [ ] A conflict or disagreement and how you handled it
- [ ] A time you improved a process / made something more efficient (manufacturing gold)
- [ ] Working with a team / stakeholders under constraints

---

## Career-change questions you *will* get — prepare these

- **"Why are you moving from manufacturing to AI/ML?"** → Have a crisp, positive story. Show pull toward AI (you're already building it), not just push away from the old job.
- **"You don't have a CS degree — how do you handle the fundamentals?"** → Point to this repo, your foundations work, and your shipped projects. Confidence, not apology.
- **"Tell me about something you built."** → Walk through a project end-to-end: problem → approach → tradeoffs → result.

---

## Also prepare

- **Your questions for them.** Always have 3–4 thoughtful questions ready — it signals genuine interest.
- **The 30-second intro.** A tight "who I am and why I'm here" for "tell me about yourself."

---

## Practice loop

1. Write each story with the template.
2. Say it out loud and time it (~2 min).
3. Trim filler; sharpen the numbers.
4. Do a mock with a friend or record yourself in Week 12.
